Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 27, 2026

Data controller: Paulius Petrusauskas, Vilnius, Lithuania

This policy explains how Paulius Petrusauskas (the “Developer”, “I”) handles information across all mobile applications (the “Apps”) I publish on the Apple App Store. It applies to every App unless that App shows a different policy. Many of my Apps are private by default and work entirely on your device; some Apps show ads or use analytics, as described below. Where a section is marked “for Apps that show ads or use analytics,” it applies only to those Apps.

Who I am

I am the data controller for the processing described here. I am an individual developer based in Vilnius, Lithuania. You can reach me using the contact details at the end of this policy.

No account required

The Apps do not require you to create an account or to provide your name, email address, or other directly identifying information to function.

Information stored on your device

Preferences, settings, and any content or answers you enter are stored locally on your device. This on-device content is not transmitted to or collected by me, and it is removed when you delete the App. This does not apply to the identifiers and usage data collected by the third-party services described below, which are transmitted off your device and are not necessarily deleted when you delete the App.

Subscriptions and purchases

Subscriptions and in-app purchases are processed by Apple. Payment is handled entirely by Apple; I do not receive or store your payment details. I receive only the subscription status needed to unlock paid features. Apple processes this information under Apple’s Privacy Policy.

Third-party services (for Apps that show ads or use analytics)

Some Apps use third-party services that process limited technical and usage data to operate the App, measure performance, deliver or measure advertising, and process subscriptions. Each processes data under its own privacy policy and, for advertising, may act as an independent controller — so you should also consult its policy to exercise rights over that data. Depending on the App, these may include:

  • Apple — App Store delivery and subscription processing. See apple.com/legal/privacy.
  • Google AdMob — serving and measuring advertising. See Google’s Privacy Policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services.
  • Google Firebase — app stability and, where used, analytics. See firebase.google.com/support/privacy.
  • Advertising, analytics, or attribution providers used by a given App — see that App’s App Store privacy details for the specific providers and data.

Data processed by these services typically includes device and advertising identifiers, IP address, general device and system information, approximate location derived from IP, and interaction events. It does not include the on-device content described above.

Advertising and tracking (for Apps that show ads)

Where an App shows ads, advertising services may access your device’s advertising identifier (on iOS, the IDFA) and similar identifiers to serve and measure ads. On iOS, before any tracking, you are shown Apple’s App Tracking Transparency (ATT) prompt. If you decline, or if you have turned off “Allow Apps to Request to Track” in Settings, you will still see ads, but they will be non-personalized and your IDFA will not be used to track you across apps and websites. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. In the EEA and UK, where consent is required, an App will also ask for your consent to non-essential advertising or analytics before those services are used — this is separate from, and in addition to, the ATT prompt, and you can withdraw it at any time in the App’s settings. Where an App is directed to or likely used by children, ads are configured as non-personalized regardless of these settings.

Legal bases

Where the GDPR applies, I rely on: your consent for advertising and analytics identifiers (Art. 6(1)(a)); performance of a contract to provide subscriptions and unlock paid features (Art. 6(1)(b)); and my legitimate interests in the basic operation, security, and stability of the Apps (Art. 6(1)(f)).

Data retention

On-device data remains until you delete it or uninstall the App. Data held by third-party providers is retained under their own policies. I retain subscription-status information only for as long as needed to provide paid features and to meet legal (for example, accounting) obligations.

International data transfers

Some third-party providers (for example Apple and Google) are located in, or transfer data to, the United States and other countries outside the European Economic Area. Where this happens, the transfer relies on an adequacy decision (such as the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, where the provider is certified) or, otherwise, on the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses with additional safeguards. You can ask me for more information, or consult each provider’s privacy policy.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access your personal data, and to its rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, and portability, as well as the right to object to processing based on legitimate interests and to withdraw any consent you have given at any time. To exercise these rights, contact me using the details below; I will respond within one month. Deleting the App removes the content stored on your device; it does not delete data already collected by advertising or analytics providers, so to exercise choices over that data please also use the controls above or the relevant provider’s policy.

Complaints

You have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority. In Lithuania this is the State Data Protection Inspectorate (Valstybinė duomenų apsaugos inspekcija, VDAI, vdai.lrv.lt). You may also complain to the authority in your EU country of residence.

Your US privacy choices

Use of advertising identifiers to serve personalized ads may be considered a “sale” or “sharing” of personal information under some US state laws. You can opt out on iOS via App Tracking Transparency (see above) and, on Android where applicable, by resetting or deleting your advertising ID in device settings.

Children’s privacy

The Apps are general-audience products and are not directed to children, and I do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

  • In the United States, the Apps are not directed to children under 13 (Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act).
  • In the EEA and UK, the minimum age to consent to processing that requires consent (such as personalized advertising) is 16, or the lower age set by your country’s law (in Lithuania, 14). Below that age you may use an App only with the involvement of a holder of parental responsibility.
  • Where an App is directed to, or likely to be used by, children, ads shown in it are non-personalized and advertising identifiers are not used to track those users.

If you believe a child has provided personal information contrary to this policy, contact me and I will delete it.

Automated decision-making

I do not carry out automated decision-making that produces legal or similarly significant effects.

Changes
